Settings Page

Where an app's settings live: a list of their parts beside the one that's open, fields that save together, switches that take effect at once, and what can't be undone kept apart at the end. Made only of Canon's components. Change a field and try to leave, save, flip a switch, or try to delete the workspace.

The pattern

An App Shell whose page is the settings (its sidebar a rail, for the room): a Page Header, then the settings' own Nav List beside the part that's open (above it, in a row, when there isn't room for both). In an app, each part is its own address: /settings/general, /settings/notifications.

The settings' parts

A Nav List named "Settings", beside the part that's open: links, because each part is its own address that people share, bookmark and come back to. Not Tabs (those switch views of one thing in place), and not one long page (people come for one setting). The open part is aria-current="page", and opening one moves focus to its title, as a new page would.

The page's Page Header says whose settings these are; each part is a Section, its title one level under the page's (h2).

Two ways to save

Fields that belong together save together. General is a Form Layout with data-layout="aside" (each section's title in a side column, its fields beside it) that ends in a Save Bar: it counts the changes, keeps Save unavailable until there are some, saves with ⌘S, and asks before the changes are lost, here when leaving for another part.

A setting on its own takes effect at once. Notifications are Switches: flipping one is the change, and a Toast says so. No Save button, no "unsaved changes".

Never both in one part: a switch beside fields that wait for Save leaves people unsure what's saved. Within a form that saves, a yes-or-no is a Checkbox (Add late fees), not a Switch.

The danger zone

What can't be undone goes last, apart from the form, in a section that says so: a Description List whose items each say what happens and end with their Button. Deleting asks in an alert Dialog that names what goes (the workspace, its invoices, its customers), and only deletes once the workspace's name is typed: the button stays unavailable (aria-disabled) until then.

Narrow

Without room for both, the settings' Nav List goes above the part, its links in a row, so the form keeps its width. Narrower still, the aside layout puts each section's title over its fields (Form Layout does that by itself below 40rem).
