Pending

Canon builds tables, forms and dialogs once, in plain HTML: the browser does the work, a theme decides the look, and the markup outlives both.

  1. Write it once, keep it

    A <button> written today still works in ten years. Canon is unopinionated about your stack and your look: it's markup, so it works with whatever renders it and outlives the framework of the moment.

  2. Accessible by nature

    This is where Canon is opinionated: names, roles, focus and keys are part of each component's markup. No one has to remember them, and no theme can lose them.

  3. Less JavaScript every year

    Browsers keep doing more on their own. Canon uses what they do, and its script steps back as they catch up.

  4. Works the way you expect

    A table should read like a table, a form should never lose what was typed, and the keyboard should always work.

Not included

What Canon leaves to others, on purpose.

A JavaScript component library
There's nothing to import or call, and no build step. Your framework or server renders the HTML.
Locked to its look
The look lives in one stylesheet. Change its colors, or replace it with your own theme, and no page changes.
Utility classes
Markup says what a thing is (data-intent), never how it looks: no “outline”, “rounded” or “blue”.
An app framework
No routing, data loading or state. canon.js keeps the interface honest; saving and fetching are your app's.
For marketing sites
Tables, forms, records and dashboards. Hero sections and pricing tables are out of scope.
A pile of effects
Motion in the components explains a change, like a tab's indicator sliding across, or it isn't there.

Origins

The name comes from fiction, where canon is the body of work officially accepted as authoritative within a story's universe. Here, the markup is canon. Themes can retell it however they like, but what the markup says stays true in all of them.

In 2003, Dave Shea published CSS Zen Garden: one HTML document, and a gallery of designs that changed nothing but the stylesheet. Each looked like a different site, and the markup never moved.

Canon is an homage to that page. It asks the same of the things internal tools are made of: write a table, a form or a dialog once, then let a theme redraw all of it without touching a line of HTML.
